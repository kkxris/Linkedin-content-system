# Strategy Examples — Post Ideas by Content Pillar

Reference descriptions of the drafted posts already in the Notion "Draft posts" database, organized by content pillar. These describe what each post is about, its angle, and its takeaway — not the finished post copy. For the actual written posts, see the Notion database.

## Content Pillar: AI + Work

**Idea 1: Why AI suddenly "forgets" things mid-conversation**
Explains the concept of an AI model's context window using the analogy of a restaurant table that's run out of room. The post breaks down what fills up context (messages, documents, instructions, conversation history) and why hitting that limit causes an AI to drop earlier details, miss instructions, or contradict something discussed earlier. Angle: make a technical limitation feel intuitive rather than mysterious or broken.

**Idea 2: AI's double-edged role in cybersecurity, using a real incident**
Uses a real event — a critical PHP vulnerability that took down government sites and affected Wikipedia — to make the point that AI helped researchers find the vulnerability, which is both impressive and unsettling. Angle: AI speeds up both sides of security, finding vulnerabilities and potentially exploiting them, so the "AI makes everything safer" narrative is incomplete.

**Idea 3: Reframing "AI makes us lazy" as a feature, not a flaw**
Takes the common criticism that AI is making people lazy and flips it: being "lazy" about low-value, repetitive work (drafting emails, summarizing documents) frees up mental energy for thinking, creating, and deciding. Angle: a contrarian take that reframes a criticism as a sign of getting pickier about where effort goes.

**Idea 4: Head-to-head test of 4 AI models on the same design brief**
Describes an experiment giving four different AI models the identical design brief and comparing results — one nailed it, one was pretty but impractical, one surprised, one missed entirely. Angle: practical, experiment-based content that shifts the question from "which AI is prettiest" to "which AI actually understands why a design works."

**Idea 5: AI models reviewing and correcting each other's work**
Describes a workflow where one AI does a task, a second AI critiques and sends it back for revision, and a third checks the final version — effectively AI "having meetings" with other AI. Angle: light, slightly absurd framing of multi-agent/agentic workflows, ending on the self-aware joke of becoming the person who just shows up for the final meeting.

**Idea 6: Gen AI vs Agentic AI, explained simply**
A plain-language breakdown of the difference between generative AI (you ask, it creates the thing) and agentic AI (you give a goal, it plans and executes multiple steps toward an outcome), using a launch email example for each. Angle: demystify a trending but often jargon-heavy distinction with a simple side-by-side.

**Idea 7: Why "hours saved" isn't real AI ROI**
Argues that AI ROI isn't about time saved on a spreadsheet, it's about what that time or capacity actually converts into — more deals closed, better campaigns, more tickets handled, faster analysis. Gives concrete before/after-style examples across sales, marketing, support, and founders. Angle: a corrective to vanity AI-adoption metrics, grounded in business outcomes.

**Idea 8: Fine-tuning explained through the frustration of repeating yourself to AI**
Opens with the relatable annoyance of giving an AI the same style/tone instructions over and over, then introduces fine-tuning as "teaching the model what good looks like" through examples rather than repeated prompting. Frames prompting vs fine-tuning as "telling vs showing." Angle: educational post structured around a familiar pain point.

**Idea 9: RAG explained as an "open book exam" for AI**
A simple, slightly playful explainer of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) — instead of expecting an AI to know everything, you give it access to your own docs/knowledge base so it can look things up before answering. Positioned as a natural follow-up to the fine-tuning explainer ("fine tuning = learn how to behave, RAG = here's the information you need"). Angle: back-to-school framing for a technical concept.

**Idea 10: AI hallucinations — the danger of confident wrong answers**
Explains AI hallucination: the model can state a fake statistic, invented source, or polished-sounding wrong answer with total confidence, because it's generating the statistically likely answer, not verified fact. Core takeaway: "confident ≠ correct." Angle: a caution/education post aimed at building healthier skepticism toward AI output.

## Content Pillar: Marketing + Content

**Idea 1: Posting more content won't fix unclear positioning**
Makes the case that teams posting daily without being able to explain, in one sentence, what makes them different, have a positioning problem, not a volume problem. Angle: contrarian pushback against the "just post more" instinct, redirecting effort toward clarifying the core message before adding to the calendar.

**Idea 2: Nobody stops to ask if content is actually worth saying**
Observes that teams have gotten very good at the mechanics of content production, briefs, drafts, approvals, but rarely stop to question whether the underlying idea is worth sharing at all. Angle: a reflective, slightly self-critical industry observation aimed at marketers and content teams specifically.

**Idea 3: AI is making content sound the same, and that's the real risk**
Notes that content increasingly uses the same hooks, hot takes, and polished-but-generic paragraphs, accelerated by AI making it easy to produce more of it. Argues the differentiator was never production speed; it's having something genuinely different to say. Angle: positions original thinking as the actual competitive advantage in an AI-saturated content landscape.

**Idea 4: A full content calendar isn't a strategy**
Distinguishes between a calendar (what to post, and when) and a strategy (why anyone should care). Makes the point that a team can be perfectly consistent and still invisible, because consistency organizes output but doesn't create a reason to pay attention. Angle: a clarifying distinction for teams who equate "being organized" with "having a strategy."

**Idea 5: The volume myth — why posting more isn't a growth strategy**
Challenges the "post more to grow more" instinct directly, using a personal admission of once believing it too, then describing watching accounts post daily and go nowhere while one well-placed post outperformed a month of filler. Core line: "frequency without resonance is just noise with a schedule." Angle: experience-based correction of a common growth myth.

**Idea 6: "Bold, friendly, authentic" isn't a brand voice, it's a vibe**
Pokes at generic brand-voice adjectives (bold, friendly, authentic) as being compliments rather than actionable instructions, and argues a real voice guide needs concrete rules: sentence length, words to avoid, where humor goes, what gets cut. Angle: practical critique aimed at marketers who've written or received a vague brand voice doc.

**Idea 7: Automating a broken content process just produces bad content faster**
Drawn from building SocialFlo, this post argues that AI doesn't fix unclear strategy or fuzzy positioning, it just helps you produce more of the same output at higher speed. Core line: "fix the thinking before you automate the doing." Angle: a founder-lesson-flavored marketing post warning against using AI as a shortcut around strategic clarity.

**Idea 8: Going viral and being trusted are not the same win**
Distinguishes between reach (loud, temporary, a number) and trust (quiet, compounding, a customer), using the observation of posts that hit huge reach but generated zero actual business impact. Angle: reframes what "success" should mean for business-driven content, not just vanity metrics.

**Idea 9: Your audience doesn't reward effort, only relevance**
Makes the point that readers never see the hours spent drafting and revising, they only see the post, and they judge it purely on "does this matter to me." Angle: a direct, slightly blunt reminder for creators who equate effort invested with content quality.

**Idea 10: Marketing teams' real problem is fragmented tools, not a lack of them**
Describes the "seven tabs problem": research in one place, writing in another, approvals elsewhere, reporting nowhere, and argues that adding another tool to that stack doesn't fix the fragmentation, it just adds an eighth tab. Angle: a workflow/systems-focused critique aimed at marketing teams drowning in tool sprawl.

## Content Pillar: Building + Founder Journey

**Idea 1: Almost hiring a developer before learning to build it herself**
Describes nearly hiring a developer to build the first SocialFlo prototype, then building it herself instead with Claude Code, and realizing that if she'd hired first she'd have paid someone to build her own confusion. Takeaway: understanding exactly what needs building, and why, was the part that was actually expensive to skip. Angle: a specific, personal founder lesson about learning by doing before delegating.

**Idea 2: What founder life actually looks like day to day**
Contrasts the glamorous image of founder life (big vision, bold moves) with the real, unremarkable middle: fixing bugs, testing fixes, texting a user to ask if something feels weird, rewriting the same onboarding flow repeatedly. Angle: a grounding, honest behind-the-scenes post about the gap between the founder narrative and the daily reality.

**Idea 3: Deciding to stop being hyper-responsive in order to actually build**
A personal admission of having once taken pride in replying to every message within the hour, then realizing that was optimizing for responsiveness, not progress. Lists specific habits stopped (answering every DM instantly, joining every call, saying yes to every coffee chat). Angle: a reflective post on founder growth as learning to do less, on purpose.

**Idea 4: When "simple" onboarding backfired**
Describes building an onboarding flow so minimal that users didn't know what to do, losing them, then rebuilding it with actual step-by-step guidance, which raised satisfaction. Takeaway: she had been optimizing for the product looking smart, when users just wanted to know where to click. Angle: a specific product-building lesson about designing for people, not for cleverness.

**Idea 5: Not waiting to feel "ready" before building SocialFlo**
Recounts not knowing how to code four months before building SocialFlo, and choosing to start asking Claude Code beginner questions rather than waiting to learn first, breaking and fixing things along the way. Core line: "ready is a myth founders wait around for, doing is the thing that actually makes you ready." Angle: a direct, personal story aimed at readers who feel blocked by not feeling qualified yet.

**Idea 6: What actually signals a healthy community (it's not turnout)**
Uses Co-Work & growing to 160+ people after six sessions, but argues the real signal wasn't the initial turnout, it was the quieter one: people returning without being asked (nearly 40% repeat attendance at one workshop). Takeaway: community is a habit people choose to keep, not a single event. Angle: data-grounded reflection on community building.

**Idea 7: Compliments aren't validation — the uncomfortable lesson about paying customers**
Describes realizing that loving an idea and being willing to pay for it are two very different signals, after spending weeks collecting compliments and mistaking them for validation. Takeaway: ask about money early, even when it feels rude, especially when it feels rude. Angle: a candid, slightly self-critical founder lesson about distinguishing enthusiasm from demand.

**Idea 8: How Claude Code changed how many bad ideas she's willing to test**
Contrasts the old cost of testing an idea (weeks, a developer, a queue) with the new reality of testing something in an afternoon and discarding it by dinner if it's not working. Takeaway: cheap failure means more chances to find the good ideas. Angle: a build-in-public reflection on how AI tooling changed her actual process, not just her output.

**Idea 9: The comment that changed how she runs every workshop since**
Recounts a participant quietly admitting after a session, "I didn't think I was smart enough to build something with AI," and how that reframed her approach to teaching confidence first, tools second, because the real blocker was never technical. Angle: an emotionally grounded teaching-philosophy story anchored in a specific real moment.

**Idea 10: An honest, unresolved reflection on how much of the business should run without her**
A more open-ended, in-progress post about trying to figure out how much of SocialFlo should depend on her directly versus the system she's built, explicitly framed as "not a lesson, not solved yet." Angle: a vulnerable, process-over-polish post that resists the pressure to wrap every founder story in a tidy takeaway.

## Content Pillar: Community + Education

**Idea 1: Watching an idea turn into a room full of people**
Reflects on Co-Work & starting as a messy note on her phone, written out of frustration with how hard it was to meet people in Berlin who wanted to build something rather than just swap business cards, and now being 160+ people later. Angle: an origin-story reflection on how building a community doesn't feel like strategy while it's happening.

**Idea 2: Why generic Berlin networking wasn't working, and what she built instead**
Describes the familiar cycle of Berlin networking events (name tags, small talk, a stack of cards never looked at again) producing contacts instead of relationships, and starting Co-Work & as a room where people actually work on something together first. Angle: a pain-point-led origin story explaining the "why" behind the format.

**Idea 3: What happens when you put strangers in a room with a shared deadline**
Describes the specific dynamic that emerges when 20 strangers are given a deadline together: people start helping each other, fixing each other's work, realizing they're solving the same problem from different angles. Takeaway: you can't plan those moments directly, you can only build the room where they're allowed to happen. Angle: an observational, almost anthropological look at what makes the format work.

**Idea 4: The invisible chaos behind a smooth-looking event**
Pulls back the curtain on what it actually takes to run an event that looks effortless: last-minute venue cancellations, delayed speakers, failing wifi, a midnight seating-plan rebuild, none of which attendees ever see. Angle: a behind-the-scenes post that builds trust by showing the real operational effort behind a polished result.

**Idea 5: "Free" gets people through the door, connection brings them back**
Reflects on initially assuming the event topic or speaker was the main draw, then noticing the same faces returning and realizing what people actually valued was the people they met and the conversations that stayed with them. Angle: a shift-in-thinking post about what actually retains community members over time.

**Idea 6: Why workshops are built around doing, not watching**
Notes that many people watch AI tutorials but never actually open the terminal themselves, comparing it to watching cooking videos and calling yourself a chef. Describes building workshops where people build something live, with help the moment they get stuck. Takeaway: confidence isn't taught, it's built one unstuck moment at a time. Angle: a philosophy post explaining the hands-on workshop format's reasoning.

**Idea 7: What a sold-out workshop actually signals**
Reframes a sold-out Claude Code workshop (filled in four days, created a waitlist) not as a marketing win but as a sign that attendees are quietly afraid of being left behind by AI and don't know where to start. Angle: a reflective, slightly provocative reframing of a typical "proof of demand" brag post into something more emotionally honest.

**Idea 8: The best workshop moment wasn't on the agenda**
Describes an unplanned moment at a workshop where one participant quietly helped a stranger who'd gotten stuck, unprompted, and notes that this kind of moment can't be designed into a run-of-show, only enabled by the room. Angle: a short, warm observational post about what actually makes people want to come back.

**Idea 9: Building sessions people regret missing more than attending**
Describes the realization that everyone invited to a Co-Work & session already had a calendar that said no, so instead of competing for time she focused on building sessions people would feel worse about skipping than attending: focused work, real connection, something they'd actually finish. Angle: a positioning-style post about designing for genuine value instead of chasing attention.

**Idea 10: An honest recap of what's worked and what hasn't with Co-Work &**
A more candid, in-progress reflection acknowledging that not everything tried with Co-Work & has worked, some ideas and formats flopped quietly, and that what surprised her most wasn't what worked but how much repeat attendance (almost 40% at one workshop) mattered more than first impressions. Angle: a transparent, still-figuring-it-out recap rather than a polished highlight reel.
