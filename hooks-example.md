# Hooks Example — How to Write and Select Vidushi's Hooks

This file is the reference for hook creation, selection, language and examples. `Voice.md` points here for all of it.

## Before Writing a Hook

Before writing a hook, understand the actual post and find its most specific, human, unexpected detail, then express it in Vidushi's voice: nonchalant, smart, slightly quirky, classy, conversational, and not overly polished. Hooks should create a small "wait, what?" or "tell me more" reaction without giving away the whole lesson. Prefer specific experiences, observations, strange but accurate analogies, confessions, or unexpected details over generic AI/LinkedIn formulas. Avoid anything that sounds templated, overly corporate, motivational, or AI generated, and don't force humour or quirkiness. The hook must naturally match the post, use only information actually provided, stay short and clean, avoid hyphens, and never feel like something that could belong to any random LinkedIn creator.

---

## No Invented Specifics

Never invent:

- Numbers
- Results
- Experiences
- Failures
- Customer reactions
- Workshop outcomes
- Quotes
- Events
- Personal experiences
- Business results

If a number or detail is not provided in the source material, do not create one just to make the hook stronger.

Specificity must come from the actual source material.

---

## Formatting Rules

- No hyphens in LinkedIn post copy.
- Keep hooks short and clean.
- Avoid unnecessary setup.
- Avoid overly polished corporate language.
- Use emojis only when they genuinely fit.
- Do not force humour.
- Do not force a quirky line into every hook.
- Do not use the same hook structure repeatedly.

## Approved Hooks (Reference Examples)

These are reference examples, not templates to reuse mechanically. Create a new hook for the specific post, grounded in its actual point and facts.

1. **"Are you all seeing this, or am I hallucinating? Why does every piece of content sound the same lately?"**
2. **"You can have 30 posts planned and still have absolutely nothing worth saying."**
3. **"Your audience isn't handing out medals for effort."**
4. **"I think we've collectively forgotten to ask, 'Wait, should we even be doing this?' And it's showing."**
5. **"Four months before building SocialFlo, I couldn't code. So naturally, I decided to build an AI product."**
6. **"I want SocialFlo to run on systems. Unfortunately, the system is me in several places."**
7. **"I made onboarding so simple that users had no idea what to do."**
8. **"The most dangerous thing about AI isn't that it can be wrong. It's that it can be wrong with confidence."**
9. **"Your AI probably isn't forgetting you. You just gave it too much to remember."**
10. **"The term 'Free' gets people through the door. What brings them back is a different story."**

### "Berlin has a funny way of introducing you to people you didn't know you needed to meet. 👀"

Why it works:
- Conversational
- Specific to Berlin/community
- Doesn't explain the lesson
- Creates curiosity
- Feels like something a person would actually say

### "Somewhere between 37 open tabs and a coffee that's gone cold, you convinced yourself you were being productive. 🫠"

Why it works:
- Specific visual
- Slightly bizarre
- Relatable
- Human
- Not a standard LinkedIn formula

### "Not every room changes your perspective. But the right one can change the questions you ask."

Why it works:
- Simple
- Classy
- Quietly provocative
- Doesn't try too hard
- Fits community/event content

### "Your fifth productivity tool is not going to save you, babe. 💅 A better system might."

Why it works:
- Has personality
- Slightly cheeky
- Unexpected
- Sounds like a person
- Strong founder voice

Use this kind of sass selectively. Do not make every hook sound like this.

### "I used to think having a plan meant I was making progress. Turns out, a beautifully organised to do list can still be a very elegant form of procrastination. 🎯"

Why it works:
- Personal
- Specific
- Unexpected phrasing
- Has a natural observation
- The idea unfolds rather than immediately stating a lesson

## Hooks to Avoid (Rejected / Needs Rework)

These were drafted, reviewed, and rejected or flagged for rework. Kept here so the same mistakes aren't repeated.

### 1. "For a year, I thought I had to become a developer to teach AI..."

**Reason:** The first line has potential, but the second line feels too polished and AI generated. The information is not necessarily the problem; the **tone and phrasing** are.

**Lesson:** A genuine founder story can still sound AI generated if the wording becomes overly neat or inspirational.

### 2. "The biggest thing holding your team back from AI isn't the tools..."

**REMOVE.**

**Reason:** Too obviously follows the "X isn't the problem. Y is the problem" LinkedIn formula.

**Lesson:** Avoid generic invisible villain / problem vs. solution framing unless there is a genuinely unusual way to express it.

### 3. "Most companies don't need an AI strategy. They need their marketing manager to stop being afraid of a terminal."

**REMOVE / REWORK.**

**Reason:** The first line sounds like a very common AI thought leadership pattern. The idea may be useful, but the framing needs to be more quirky and specific.

### 4. "30 seats. Sold out in under 48 hours. No ads, no cold outreach..."

**REMOVE / REWORK.**

**Reason:** The opening has useful specificity, but the ending feels manufactured and overly "LinkedIn case study."

Also, never use hyphens.

### 5. "We ran a Claude Code workshop for people who'd never opened a terminal. [X]% said..."

**REMOVE / REWORK.**

**Reason:** The underlying proof may be useful, but the language sounds AI generated. The hook needs to sound more natural and specific to the actual experience.

Do not use placeholder numbers like `[X]%` unless the actual number is known and approved.

### 6. "If your team disappeared for a week, would your AI tools keep working..."

**REMOVE / REWORK.**

**Reason:** The uncomfortable question structure is very LinkedIn and feels constructed.

### 7. "One sentence in your prompt is probably costing you hours every week..."

**REMOVE / REWORK.**

**Reason:** Generic consequence hook. It could be written for almost any AI/productivity post.

### 8. "The best workshop feedback I ever got wasn't..."

**REMOVE / REWORK.**

**Reason:** The underlying story/detail is interesting, but the setup follows a familiar "the best feedback wasn't X, it was Y" LinkedIn pattern.

The actual feedback may still be valuable. Find a more natural and unexpected way into it.

### 9. "A corporate L&D team offered to 10x our workshop fee..."

**REMOVE / REWORK.**

**Reason:** The opening feels like a manufactured LinkedIn story hook. The situation itself may be interesting, but the framing should sound more like Vidushi and less like a case study headline.

## Final Hook Selection Test

Before approving a hook, check:

**1. Is it specific?**
Could this hook only realistically come from Vidushi's experience, observation, or perspective?

**2. Is it human?**
Would someone actually say this in conversation?

**3. Is it unexpected?**
Does it create a small "wait, what?" moment?

**4. Is it too LinkedIn?**
Does it sound like a template, motivational post, thought leadership formula, or AI generated copy?

**5. Does it match the body?**
Does the hook naturally lead into the actual post?

**6. Is it concise?**
Can anything be removed without making it weaker?

**7. Is it grounded?**
Are all claims and specifics actually supported by the source material?

If a hook fails the **"too LinkedIn / too AI"** test, do not just replace one or two words.

**Change the angle.**

The goal is not to make a generic hook sound slightly less generic.

The goal is to find the **interesting human detail or unexpected angle that makes the hook feel like Vidushi.**
