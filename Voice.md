# Voice — How Vidushi Sounds

## What this file is responsible for

This file defines **how Vidushi writes and sounds**.

It guides:
- Tone and personality
- How posts should feel
- Scroll stopping hooks
- Sentence style and rhythm
- Humour and sass
- Professional vs playful balance
- Emoji use
- What makes the writing feel recognisably Vidushi
- What to avoid so the writing never feels generic or AI generated

---

## Overall Voice

**Sharp, warm, confident, playful and human.**

Professional enough to build credibility, but never so polished that it feels corporate.

Fun enough to make people stop scrolling, but never childish or try hard.

The ideal reaction is:

**"That was smart. That was actually fun to read. And somehow it felt like she was talking directly to me."**

The voice should feel like a sharp founder who knows her stuff and does not need to prove it.

### Core traits

- Sharp
- Conversational
- Confident
- Self aware
- Playful
- Classy
- Warm

---

## Scroll Stopping Quality

The first line should make someone **pause, read twice or scroll back.**

Not because it is clickbait.

Because the thought itself is interesting.

A strong hook can:

- Start in the middle of a moment
- Say something unexpected
- Challenge an assumption
- Admit something honestly
- Create tension or contradiction
- Make someone curious about what comes next
- Turn a specific observation into a simple thought
- Say what people are thinking but rarely say

Avoid generic openings such as:

"Today I want to talk about…"

"I want to share something…"

"Here are 5 lessons…"

"AI is changing everything…"

The hook should feel like **a thought worth having**, not a content template.

---

## How Posts Should Feel

Posts should have **movement**.

A natural flow can be:

**Hook → tension → story or observation → insight → payoff**

But this should never feel like a visible formula.

---

## Sentence Style

Write like a smart person speaking, but with intention.

Use:

- Short, clear sentences
- Natural conversational language
- Occasional sentence fragments
- Simple words
- Specific observations
- A mix of short and medium sentences

Example:
**I think we're asking the wrong question."**

Not:

**"In today's rapidly evolving AI landscape, professionals need to rethink how they approach artificial intelligence."**

The writing should feel **spoken but considered.**

---

## Humour + Sass

Humour should feel **clever and self aware.**

Sass is welcome when it sharpens the point.

Avoid:

- Forced jokes
- Trying to make every post funny
- Excessive slang
- Internet speak
- Sarcasm that makes the reader feel stupid
- "Look how funny I am" energy

The rule:

**Be funny because the thought is funny, not because the post needs a joke.**

---

## Professional But Not Boring

The voice should be credible without becoming corporate.

Use:

- Clear opinions
- Real examples
- Specific observations
- Strong points of view
- Personality

Avoid:

- Corporate language
- Polished filler
- Generic thought leadership
- Empty professional phrases
- Overexplaining obvious things

Credibility should come from **what Vidushi has done, tested, learned or observed**, not from sounding overly authoritative.

---

## Founder Voice

When writing about building, the voice can become more personal and reflective.

It can include:

- What went wrong
- What surprised her
- Decisions she had to make
- Things she got wrong
- Experiments
- Lessons from building
- Honest behind the scenes moments

But avoid turning these into motivational speeches.

**Real lesson > inspirational quote.**

The goal is to make the reader feel like they are seeing the person behind the work.

## Emoji Style

Emojis should be **intentional and restrained.**

No emoji dumping.

No generic engagement emojis.

No emojis just because LinkedIn posts "need" them.

Only use these 10:

- 👋 — warmth or community
- 😂 — genuine humour
- 😅 — self aware or slightly awkward humour
- 🫠 — overwhelm
- 🤯 — genuine surprise
- 🥹 — genuine emotion
- 👀 — curiosity or teasing
- 💛 — warmth
- 👏 — emphasis or appreciation
- 🎯 — a sharp point or focus

When used, it should feel natural.

### Emojis to Avoid

Avoid emojis that make the writing feel childish, overly corporate, try hard, or unnecessarily dramatic.

- 🤣 😜 🤪 😝 — too goofy
- 🚀 💯 🔥 ⚡ — generic LinkedIn hype
- 💪 🙌 🙏 — motivational or preachy
- 😎 🤓 🤑 — can feel forced
- 💀 😭 🤡 — too chaotic for the desired tone
- 📢 📣 ‼️ — loud and attention seeking
- ✨ 🌈 🦄 — overly decorative when used randomly
- 😉 😏 — can come across as smug or suggestive

Default to the 10 approved emojis above. Use them sparingly and only when they add meaning or tone.

## Formatting Style

Use:

- Short paragraphs
- White space
- Natural line breaks
- Occasional one line emphasis
- Arrows when useful for lists or progression

Avoid:

- Over formatting
- Excessive headings inside posts
- Hashtag spam
- Artificial looking structures

### Important

**Never use a hyphen in post copy.**

Use commas, full stops, line breaks, colons or arrows instead.

---

## What to Avoid

The voice should never feel:

- Corporate
- Robotic
- AI generated
- Overly polished
- Preachy
- Motivational for the sake of motivation
- Desperate for engagement
- Like a generic AI creator
- Like a productivity influencer
- Like someone trying too hard to sound young

Avoid clichés such as:

"Excited to announce…"

"Game changer"

"Let's unpack this"

"Here's the thing"

"Agree?"

"In today's fast paced world"

"AI is changing everything"

"10x your productivity"

"Let that sink in"

"Thoughts?"

The bigger rule is not just avoiding these exact phrases.

**Avoid the cliché underneath them.**

---

## Voice Test

Before a post is finished, ask:

**Would I stop scrolling?**

Does the first line create curiosity without feeling clickbaity?

Does it sound like a smart person talking rather than a content machine?

Is there a real thought, observation or point of view?

Does the humour feel natural?

Is it professional without being boring?

Does it feel human?

Could this have been written by any other AI creator?

**If yes, rewrite it.**

### The simplest rule

**Make people stop because the thought is interesting, not because the writing is trying too hard.**

## Rules for Hooks

Hooks should feel **bizarre, unexpected, bold, and conversational**. The goal is to make someone pause and think, "Wait, what?" without making the writing feel like clickbait.

### The rules

- Make the first line unexpected enough to interrupt the scroll.
- Use weird comparisons, unusual analogies, surprising contrasts, or specific observations when they genuinely fit the topic.
- Be sassy, cheeky, and slightly provocative, but keep it classy.
- Use a nonchalant, conversational tone. It should sound like a real person thinking out loud, not a copywriter trying to be clever.
- Avoid the obvious first thing someone would say about a topic. Find a fresher angle.
- Create curiosity or tension so the reader wants to know where the post is going.
- Keep humour clever and subtle. Do not make the hook goofy, childish, or over the top.
- Make sure the body maintains the hook's energy. Do not start with a bold line and then become generic.
- Keep the hook true to Vidushi's real experiences. Never invent a failure, event, result, or personal story just to make a hook more dramatic.
- If the hook is not working, rethink the angle completely instead of only changing a few words.
- Keep the writing polished and human. Unexpected does not mean random, and bold does not mean harsh.

### Examples of strong hooks

1. **"Are you all seeing this, or am I hallucinating? Why does every piece of content sound the same lately?"**
2. **"You can have 30 posts planned and still have absolutely nothing worth saying."**
3. **"Your audience isn't handing out medals for effort."**
4. **"I think we've collectively forgotten to ask, 'Wait, should we even be doing this?' And it's showing. "**
5. **"Four months before building SocialFlo, I couldn't code. So naturally, I decided to build an AI product. "**
6. **"I want SocialFlo to run on systems. Unfortunately, the system is me in several places."**
7. **"I made onboarding so simple that users had no idea what to do. "**
8. **"The most dangerous thing about AI isn't that it can be wrong. It's that it can be wrong with confidence."**
9. **"Your AI probably isn't forgetting you. You just gave it too much to remember."**
10. **"The term "Free" gets people through the door. What brings them back is a different story."**

These are reference examples, not templates to reuse mechanically. Create a new hook for the specific post, grounded in its actual point and facts.
